# EUA AI Academy — Step-by-Step Execution Strategy

**Programme:** EUA AI Explorers  
**Baseline date:** 31 August 2026  
**Target public launch:** 21 January 2027  
**Target first Demo Day:** 28 January 2027  
**Planning model:** 20 active delivery weeks + 2 calendar-buffer weeks

---

## Executive recommendation

Run the programme as a gated launch, not as a fixed-date content project. The critical path is:

> governance approval → legally valid consent design → four-lesson pilot → production format lock → 20-lesson production → LMS and community integration → end-to-end QA → soft-launch exit gate → public launch

The earlier 20-week plan placed the soft launch across the Christmas and New Year period and scheduled the first Demo Day before the last Thursday of January. This strategy adds two calendar-buffer weeks, moves the 50–100 learner soft launch to 4–17 January, targets public launch for 21 January, and keeps the first Demo Day on Thursday, 28 January.

The launch date is a target. It is not permission to bypass a failed legal, safeguarding, content, accessibility, or platform gate.

---

## 1. Scope and launch definition

### In scope for launch

- One free, self-paced programme for ages 14–16: **AI Explorers**
- 20 bilingual lessons across 7 modules: **8 hr 27 min of video**, with each module containing **60–88 minutes**
- Armenian primary delivery with English subtitles and bilingual support copy
- 20 workbooks or practical mission sheets
- 7 optional exams with reviewed item banks
- Draft 8-badge framework and 3 certificate levels, activated only after EUA approval
- LMS progress tracking and verifiable certificate records
- Legally reviewed consent flow for learners under 16
- Moderated learner community with two trained moderators and no staff–student private messages
- Public bilingual landing page at `ai.eua.am`
- Analytics, pre/post instrument, support process, monthly Demo Day operating model

### Explicitly out of scope for this launch

- AI for Students, AI for Educators, or AI for Public Service production
- Native mobile application
- Custom-built LMS if EUA's existing LMS can meet the minimum requirements
- Automated proctoring
- Regional Demo Days at launch
- Accreditation claims that have not been formally approved
- Public claims that registration, certification, or unlimited capacity are active before the systems are ready

### Launch is complete only when

1. Legal counsel signs the privacy notice and consent flow.
2. The safeguarding lead signs the community rules and incident playbook.
3. All 20 lessons (507 edited minutes), workbooks, and required captions pass content QA and match the [detailed curriculum](./DETAILED-CURRICULUM.md).
4. Registration, consent, account creation, lesson access, exam, certificate, support, and withdrawal/deletion paths pass end-to-end testing.
5. The soft launch meets every exit criterion in Section 6.
6. A named owner is funded for the first 90 days of operations.

### Budget structure

Obtain local quotes before assigning monetary values. Approve the budget in this order:

1. **Must fund before production:** programme lead, presenter, instructional design, video production, translation/captions, legal/privacy, LMS/hosting, accessibility QA.
2. **Must fund before soft launch:** two moderators, analytics, email delivery, support coverage, certificate verification, learner testing incentives.
3. **Must fund before public launch:** campaign production, Demo Day venue/AV, event safety, permissions and photography policy.
4. **Recurring Year 1:** moderation, monthly Demo Day, platform/hosting, quarterly exam/tool review, annual re-recording of time-sensitive demonstrations.
5. **Defer if constrained:** custom mobile app, custom LMS rebuild, regional events, production of the three future programmes.

| Consent and privacy wording | Legal counsel | Technology, safeguarding, programme lead | Signed notice and tested flow |

| Public claims and campaign | Communications lead | Dean, legal, curriculum | Approved claim register and source links |

### Cadence

- **Monday:** 30-minute delivery meeting; update milestones, owners, and blockers.
- **Wednesday:** content/technology working review during W8–17.
- **Friday:** written one-page status: achieved, next, decisions, risks, budget variance.
- **At every gate:** signed go / conditional go / no-go decision with named conditions and due dates.
- **After launch:** weekly operations review for 90 days; monthly sponsor review; quarterly curriculum and risk review.

Use one source of truth for work status. Every task must have one owner, one due date, and one definition of done.

## 6. Gate criteria and success measures

### G0 — authority to proceed (end of Week 2)

- All eight open decisions have named approvers and written outcomes.
- Budget envelope and procurement path are approved.
- Presenter, programme lead, legal, safeguarding, curriculum, technology, and comms owners are named.
- Unsupported public claims are removed or marked as proposals.

### G1 — pilot-ready (end of Week 4)

- Four scripts, exercises, facilitator protocol, measurement sheet, and guardian permission are ready.
- Eight learners are recruited with the intended age, geography, device, and experience mix.
- The landing page clearly says pre-launch and does not pretend to collect enrolments.
- Pilot data collection and media permissions are approved.

### G2 — production format locked (end of Week 7)

- At least 6 of 8 pilot learners complete each practical task within the intended time.
- No unresolved critical safety, comprehension, or accessibility issue remains.
- Video, workbook, subtitles, on-screen text, and lesson timing templates are approved.
- The sponsor approves go / revise / stop based on a one-page pilot report.

### G3 — content complete (end of Week 15)

- 20 lesson masters totalling 507 edited minutes, transcripts, HY/EN captions, workbooks, and exam drafts are inventoried against the runtime plan.
- Every factual or time-sensitive product claim has a source, review date, and owner.
- Each lesson passes curriculum, language, media, privacy, and accessibility checks.
- No missing rights or permissions for media, student work, music, or third-party assets.

### G4 — end-to-end ready (end of Week 17)

- Registration → guardian confirmation → account → assessment → lesson → exam → certificate works in HY and EN.
- Withdrawal, consent revocation, data correction, and deletion/escalation paths are documented and tested.
- No critical or high accessibility issue is open.
- Moderators pass a scenario rehearsal; support coverage and escalation SLA are published internally.
- Analytics events reconcile to test accounts without collecting unnecessary personal data.

### G5 — public-launch decision (end of Week 20)

- 50–100 soft-launch learners; at least 85% complete the required consent flow.
- More than 60% start Lesson 1 within 48 hours of access.
- Registration and Lesson 1 completion have no severity-1 or unresolved severity-2 defect.
- Support acknowledgement is under one business day during the pilot.
- Fewer than 5% of users report a blocking language, device, or access problem.
- Legal, safeguarding, curriculum, technology, communications, and sponsor sign the launch record.

### First 90-day dashboard

| Area | Primary measure | Secondary diagnostic |
|---|---|---|
| Activation | Lesson 1 started within 48 hours | Registration and consent completion time |
| Learning | Pre/post change among completers | Item-level misconceptions |
| Progress | Absolute learners reaching Lessons 7 and 20 | Drop-off by lesson and device |
| Certification | Learners attempting and passing optional exams | Item difficulty and retake rate |
| Safety | Confirmed incidents by severity | Moderator response and resolution time |
| Access | Completion by region, language, and device | Caption use and technical support topics |
| Community | Demo Day attendance | Repeat attendance and submissions |

Report both absolute numbers and percentages. Do not use absolute completion alone to hide a broken funnel, and do not use completion percentage alone to undervalue broad reach.

## 7. Top dependencies and risks

| Priority | Dependency / risk | Early warning | Mitigation | Owner |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Legal consent or privacy notice is late | No approved draft by end W2 | Treat as critical path; weekly counsel checkpoint; no enrolment form before sign-off | Legal |
| 2 | LMS cannot support guardian gate or verification | Failed technical spike in W4 | Choose a supported third-party configuration or narrow credential scope; do not custom-build late | Technology |
| 3 | Presenter or production capacity slips | Presenter not confirmed by W2; master lesson fails W8 | Hold backup presenter; lock studio blocks; approve masters before batch recording | Curriculum |
| 4 | Mixed-language or inaccessible content | Subtitle backlog >1 week; mobile blockers | Parallel caption workflow; accessibility QA on first master and LMS shell | QA |
| 5 | Soft launch falls into holiday inactivity | Invitations not ready by 18 Dec | Use W18 as freeze; invite on 4 Jan; retain two-week buffer | Programme lead |
| 6 | Page promises services that are not active | “Enrol now,” unlimited capacity, or active certificates appear before approval | Maintain public claim register; comms/legal sign-off for releases | Communications |
| 7 | Community opens without operational moderation | Moderator rota or incident playbook incomplete by W17 | Do not open the learner channel; use LMS support only until ready | Safeguarding |
| 8 | Completion is low and interpreted without context | Lesson 1 activation <60% or Lesson 7 cliff | Fix activation first; publish absolute and rate metrics; run targeted learner interviews | Programme lead |
| 9 | Demo Day is treated as a marketing event only | No learner submissions or safeguarding plan by W20 | Protect learner showcase and open Q&A; rehearse event flow and permissions | Event lead |
| 10 | Time-sensitive AI tool content ages quickly | Broken interface or policy in a master lesson | Separate durable concepts from dated demonstrations; quarterly review; annual refresh budget | Curriculum |

## 8. First ten business days

1. Name the programme lead and executive sponsor.
2. Convert the eight open decisions into a signed decision log with dates.
3. Ask legal counsel to confirm Article 9 consent requirements and approve the intended electronic consent evidence.
4. Decide the LMS by capability test, not preference.
5. Confirm presenter availability through January and for the annual refresh.
6. Obtain official EUA logo, approved colours, naming, and permission to use the proposed AI Academy identity.
7. Approve the re-baselined dates and reserve studio, QA, and Demo Day capacity.
8. Define the event and data schema before configuring the LMS.
9. Recruit the eight pilot learners and prepare permissions.
10. Hold Gate G0 on 11 September; record go / conditional go / no-go and every condition owner.

## 9. Source and assurance notes

- The [official EUA website](https://www.eua.am/en/content/11) confirms the institution's public name, address (`10 Davit Anhaght Street, Yerevan`) and contact email (`info@eua.am`).
- Article 9 of Armenia's Personal Data Protection Law states that a legal representative gives consent for processing the personal data of a minor under 16. Counsel must still approve the programme's notice, evidence, retention, channel-specific consent, and withdrawal process.
- [FAST's current Generation AI information](https://www.fast.foundation/en/program/4379) describes a three-year programme beginning in Grade 10. Positioning should say **complementary and earlier-stage**, not claim a formal feeder relationship unless FAST agrees.
- The [OpenAI Education announcement](https://edunewsletter.openai.com/p/armenias-next-step-toward-ai-native) supports the May 2026 Armenia collaboration and 50,000-user access figure cited in the programme design.
- [Telegram's current terms](https://telegram.org/tos) set an 18+ minimum in the EU, UK, and Australia and do not publish an Armenia-specific minimum. Platform suitability for minors still requires EUA legal, privacy, and safeguarding review.
- “Faculty of Artificial Intelligence,” “Armenia's first university AI education ladder,” active certification, open/unlimited enrolment, and a January 2027 launch are proposed programme claims, not verified present facts. Use them publicly only after EUA approval.
